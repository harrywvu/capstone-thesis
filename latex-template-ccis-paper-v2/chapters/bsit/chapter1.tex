\section{Introduction}
\label{Introduction}

Flood-evacuation planning brings together decisions about affected communities, usable roads, shelter space, and transport. A road closure can change which center is reachable; a full center can change the route or leave some evacuees unassigned. Planners need to examine these conditions together.

This study develops \textit{OverFlow: A Geospatial Decision Support System for Flood Evacuation Planning} for Laoag City, Ilocos Norte. The system uses an interactive three-dimensional (3D) environment to help local disaster personnel configure and compare evacuation scenarios.

\subsection{Project Context}

Laoag City Hall has provided terrain, barangay, road, population, and flood-susceptibility records for the study. These layers locate communities and infrastructure, but a planning decision also depends on event-specific demand, road access, center capacity, and available transport. OverFlow connects the geographic records with those operational conditions.

The system treats population figures as references for setting evacuation demand and the supplied flood-susceptibility map as background hazard information. SFINCS provides the rainfall-driven flood-scenario method. Keeping these sources distinct allows planners to understand what a scenario represents and compare its resulting evacuation arrangements.

\subsection{Purpose and Description}

OverFlow enables planners to select any of Laoag City's 80 coded barangays, individually or in groups. Hovering highlights a barangay boundary; clicking selects or deselects it. The system presents geographic layers in 3D and overhead views.

For each scenario, the planner specifies expected evacuees, rainfall conditions, road and bridge closures, center availability and capacity, vehicle resources, and travel assumptions. The analysis identifies feasible routes and center assignments, estimates travel and vehicle requirements, and reports demand that cannot be served. Scenarios can be saved, reopened, compared, and exported for planning discussions. Modeled road exposure is presented for review; a road is excluded from routing only when the planner marks it closed.

\subsection{Objectives of the Study}

The general objective is to develop and evaluate OverFlow as a geospatial tool for configuring and comparing flood-evacuation scenarios in Laoag City. Specifically, the study aims to:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item integrate relevant terrain, barangay, road, population, center, and resource data;
  \item provide barangay-based scenario controls for demand, flooding, road access, shelter capacity, and transport;
  \item develop SFINCS flood scenarios and evaluate their local applicability;
  \item calculate feasible routes and capacity-constrained center assignments, including unserved demand and resource estimates;
  \item present and compare scenario inputs and results in an interactive geographic interface; and
  \item evaluate system functions and their usefulness in planning tasks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Significance of the Study}

For disaster planners and responders, OverFlow provides a common view of how changing road access, demand, shelter capacity, and transport affect an evacuation arrangement. Reporting unserved demand makes capacity and connectivity constraints visible. Saved scenarios support review and discussion across offices.

Residents are indirect beneficiaries of better-informed preparedness planning. The study also provides researchers and developers with an evaluated example of combining local geographic data, flood scenarios, and constrained evacuation analysis in one planning tool.

\subsection{Scope and Delimitations of the Study}

The interface covers all 80 coded Laoag barangays. Barangays 38-A Mangato East, 38-B Mangato West, and 39 Sta. Rosa form the technical SFINCS pilot area. The geographic extent of the final flood-model evaluation will follow the available terrain, boundary data, and observations from a dated flood event. Interface coverage does not imply that flood results have been validated citywide.

The study covers scenario configuration, flood modeling, geographic display, road and center analysis, travel and vehicle estimates, and scenario comparison. Population figures are reference values; the planner supplies expected evacuees and usable capacities for each scenario. Flood exposure informs review, while planner-confirmed closures determine which roads the route analysis excludes.

The supplied elevation grid has approximately 91~m cell spacing, so detailed street-level flood claims require finer terrain and local evaluation. Outputs are planning estimates under stated assumptions. The system is designed for preparedness analysis rather than real-time forecasting, public navigation, or emergency dispatch.

\subsection{Operational Definition of Terms}

\textbf{Evacuation Demand.} The expected number of evacuees specified for a selected barangay in a scenario.

\textbf{Evacuation-Center Allocation.} Assignment of evacuees to reachable centers within the usable capacities set for a scenario.

\textbf{Feasible Evacuation Route.} A road-network path from an origin to a center under the access conditions specified in a scenario.

\textbf{Flood-Evacuation Scenario.} A set of selected barangays, rainfall and demand assumptions, road access, center availability and capacity, and transport resources used to examine an evacuation arrangement.

\textbf{Unserved Demand.} Evacuees who cannot be assigned to a reachable center within the available capacity.

\textbf{Usable Capacity.} The number of evacuees a center can accommodate under the conditions specified for a scenario.

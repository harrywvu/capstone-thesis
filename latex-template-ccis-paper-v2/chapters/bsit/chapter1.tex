\section{Introduction}
\label{Introduction}

\subsection{Background of the Study}

Flood evacuation requires decisions that change with each event. The number of people needing assistance, the roads they can use, and the space available in evacuation centers may differ from one situation to another. In Laoag City, these decisions can involve communities across its 80 barangays \cite{psalaoag}. The Department of Social Welfare and Development recorded 78 Laoag barangays and 45,498 people affected by the combined effects of the Southwest Monsoon and Super Typhoon Egay in 2023 \cite{dswdegay2023}. These figures describe overall storm impacts, rather than the number of people flooded or evacuated. More recently, the Philippine Space Agency published a radar-derived map of possible flood extents from the August 2026 Southwest Monsoon \cite{philsa2026flood}.

Geographic records can help locate communities, roads, and flood-prone areas, but they do not directly answer planning questions. If a road becomes unusable, a center reaches capacity, or more residents require evacuation, planners need to know which destinations remain reachable and how many people cannot be accommodated. The Laoag datasets available for this study contain barangay boundaries, roads, population records, elevation, and flood-susceptibility information. These sources provide a basis for mapping, while evacuation demand, road access, and usable center capacity must be specified for a particular planning scenario.

These changing conditions need to be examined together. OverFlow is proposed to combine flood information with route and evacuation-center analysis. It will help planners compare alternatives, including cases where roads or listed centers cannot serve everyone.

\subsection{Statement of the Problem}

This study addresses the difficulty of determining feasible evacuation routes and center assignments when demand, road access, and center capacity change. Specifically, it asks:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item How can available Laoag geographic, population, evacuation-area, and flood information be organized for planning across all 80 barangays?
  \item How do changes in expected evacuees, road closures, and usable center capacity affect reachable routes, center assignments, and unserved demand?
  \item How well does the proposed system perform its specified planning tasks, and how do intended users assess those tasks if staff participation is obtained?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Purpose and Objectives of the Study}

The purpose of the study is to develop and evaluate a geospatial tool for preparing and comparing flood-evacuation scenarios in Laoag City. A planner will select a barangay, set the expected number of evacuees, identify closed roads, and enter the usable capacities of listed evacuation areas. The proposed system will then calculate routes by mapped road distance, assign people to reachable centers within capacity, and report those who remain unserved.

The study specifically aims to:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Assess and prepare the available Laoag data for scenario planning across its 80 barangays, including the identification of missing or uncertain inputs.
  \item Design and develop an interface for viewing geographic and flood information, configuring evacuation conditions, and comparing scenarios.
  \item Implement and test distance-based routing and capacity-limited center assignment under planner-specified road closures.
  \item Examine the feasible uses and limitations of elevation data, barangay-based SFINCS flood simulation, and dated satellite radar flood maps within the proposed system.
  \item Evaluate the planning functions through documented tests and, if participation is secured, tasks completed by intended users.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Significance of the Study}

For disaster planners, the proposed system offers a means of examining how a change in road access or center capacity affects an evacuation arrangement. Its report of unreachable locations and unserved demand can draw attention to constraints that require further planning.

Laoag residents are the intended indirect beneficiaries of more thoroughly examined evacuation options. The study may also help identify which local data require verification or updating before a geospatial planning tool can be relied upon in practice.

\subsection{Scope and Delimitations of the Study}

The study covers evacuation-scenario planning for Laoag City's 80 barangays. Its proposed functions include barangay selection, entry of evacuation demand and usable center capacities, manual road closures, route and center analysis, geographic display, and scenario comparison. Assignments will use listed evacuation areas and available road connections. Where these data do not support an assignment, the result will be reported as a gap.

The digital elevation model (DEM) provides terrain for barangay-based SFINCS simulations. The model domain may extend beyond the selected barangay to represent water entering or leaving it. Flood-susceptibility data and satellite radar (SAR) detections provide separate reference layers. Given the available terrain and event data, simulated depths will be treated as exploratory, and road closures will remain a planner input.

The study is delimited to preparedness planning and scenario comparison. It does not provide real-time forecasts, emergency dispatch, public navigation, or operational estimates of vehicle requirements and clearance time. Routes are calculated by distance under entered closures; they do not establish travel speed or safety.

\subsection{Operational Definition of Terms}

\textbf{Evacuation demand} refers to the number of people expected to evacuate from a barangay in a specified scenario.

\textbf{Usable capacity} refers to the number of evacuees a listed evacuation area can accommodate under the conditions entered for that scenario.

\textbf{Reachable route} refers to a path through the mapped road network after the planner's specified closures have been applied.

\textbf{Unserved demand} refers to evacuees who cannot be assigned to a reachable evacuation area within its usable capacity.

\textbf{DEM} refers to a grid of elevation values used to represent terrain.

\textbf{SAR flood extent} refers to an area classified as possible surface water from satellite radar data acquired at a stated time.

\textbf{SFINCS} refers to the flood-simulation method selected for barangay-based scenarios.

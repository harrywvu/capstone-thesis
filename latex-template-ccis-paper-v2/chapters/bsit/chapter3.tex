\section{Methodology}
\label{Methodology}

This chapter describes the design, data sources, instruments, procedures, and analysis used to develop and evaluate OverFlow in Laoag City.

\subsection{Research Design}

The study uses a descriptive-developmental design. Interviews document how disaster-management personnel identify affected areas, assess access and shelter capacity, and compare evacuation arrangements. An iterative development process translates these requirements into system functions and tests. The work follows the design science stages of problem identification, solution design, demonstration, and evaluation \cite{peffers2007}.

SFINCS is used for rainfall-driven flood scenarios \cite{sfincsinput,sfincsdocs,sfincsoutput}. Model setup records the terrain, rainfall, boundary conditions, parameters, and spatial and temporal resolution. A dated simulation is compared with independent flooded and dry observations before local flood-accuracy claims are made. Where this comparison is not possible, the output is presented as a technical demonstration.

Evacuation analysis excludes road links marked closed by the planner, calculates paths to reachable centers, and assigns expected evacuees within usable center capacities. NetworkX weighted paths and OR-Tools minimum-cost flow are the implementation directions. Route weights, assignment costs, and travel and vehicle assumptions are specified before testing. Results report unserved demand where access or capacity is insufficient.

Evaluation uses functional and scenario tests, followed by staff planning tasks if the partner office participates. Tests check the configured road and capacity constraints, scenario storage, and displayed results. The study does not compare OverFlow with another software system.

\subsection{Locale of the Study, Population, and Sample}

The study area is Laoag City, Ilocos Norte. The interface allows selection among all 80 coded barangays through hover highlighting and click selection. Barangays 38-A Mangato East, 38-B Mangato West, and 39 Sta. Rosa form the technical SFINCS pilot. The area used for observed-event evaluation is determined by the available flood evidence.

Laoag CDRRMO is the proposed partner for interviews and staff evaluation. Personnel involved in evacuation planning or operations will be selected purposively. Approximately six staff members form the provisional interview target; the final participant count and roles will be reported after recruitment.

\subsection{Data Gathering Tools}

Formal request letters produced an initial City Hall package, largely through the City Engineering Department, containing elevation, barangay boundaries, population, roads, and flood-susceptibility information. Additional requests seek geocoded center and capacity records, current vehicle resources, bridge locations, and observations from dated flood events. LiPAD is a prospective source of finer bare-earth terrain data.

QGIS and Python tools will be used to inspect, align, and prepare geographic layers. Roads will be cleaned and connected for network analysis, and barangay codes will link boundaries to population records. A dataset inventory will record source, date, coverage, coordinate system, completeness, and permitted use. Population counts serve as references; expected evacuees are entered separately for each scenario.

\subsection{Research Instruments}

A semi-structured interview guide covers planning steps, road closures, center choice, capacity, transport, and comparison of alternatives. A dataset checklist records source quality and preparation decisions. A system-test checklist records each scenario's inputs, expected behavior, observed output, and result.

The staff evaluation form combines task completion, a five-point rating questionnaire, and open comments. Tasks include selecting barangays, changing demand, closing a road, adjusting center capacity, and comparing scenarios. Ratings address the clarity of outputs, ease of editing inputs, and usefulness of comparisons. Instruments and task criteria will be reviewed before use.

\subsection{Data Gathering Procedures}

The team will coordinate with the proposed partner office, complete the applicable ethics review, and obtain informed consent before interviews or staff evaluation. Interviews will identify planning practices and requirements; audio will be recorded only with separate permission.

The City Hall files and any additional records will be inventoried, checked, aligned, and prepared for system use. Development then proceeds through geographic display, scenario configuration, SFINCS integration, evacuation analysis, and scenario comparison. Each function will be tested against defined inputs and expected behavior.

SFINCS evaluation will examine input alignment, numerical behavior, boundary conditions, and agreement with observations from a dated event. Without suitable terrain, boundary evidence, and observations, its maps will remain labeled technical demonstrations and will not be used to judge road safety. Planners will review modeled exposure and specify road closures for evacuation scenarios.

Functional tests will check barangay selection, closed-road avoidance, center reachability and capacity, unserved demand, and preservation of saved scenarios. Participating staff will then complete planning tasks and provide ratings and comments. The report will present dataset checks, test results, task outcomes, and staff feedback without identifying participants.

\subsection{Statistical Treatment of Data}

Interview notes and open comments will be grouped into themes and checked against the original responses. Task completion and test outcomes will be reported as counts and percentages, with the number attempted stated. Five-point ratings will be summarized by response frequency and item mean; missing responses will be excluded from that item's mean. These descriptive results will be interpreted alongside staff comments and scenario-test evidence. No inferential test or claim of superiority over another system is planned.

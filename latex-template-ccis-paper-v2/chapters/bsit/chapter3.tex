This chapter describes how the study will prepare Laoag's geographic data, develop the proposed planning functions, and evaluate their results.

\subsection{Research Design}

The study will use a descriptive-developmental design organized through design-science research. The descriptive component will examine the available data and document local evacuation-planning practices if personnel participate. The developmental component will build and evaluate a geospatial tool that responds to those requirements. Design science structures the work from problem identification and solution objectives through development, demonstration, evaluation, and communication \cite{peffers2007}.

\subsection{Software Development Process}

Within the design-science study, OverFlow will be built through iterative and incremental software development. Each iteration will plan, design, implement, test, and review a set of functions. Feedback can revise the current increment before work proceeds to the next one. The planned increments add Laoag maps and barangay selection; scenario entry, routes, and center assignment; barangay-based flood simulation and satellite-derived reference layers; then scenario comparison. Figure~\ref{fig:development-process} shows this cycle. The initial synthetic prototype demonstrates the interaction; later increments will use prepared Laoag data.

\subsection{Locale and Participants}

The study area is Laoag City, Ilocos Norte. The planning interface will include all 80 barangays. Each flood scenario will focus on a selected barangay, with the SFINCS domain extending beyond its boundary where required to represent incoming or outgoing water. Figure~\ref{fig:laoag-setting} shows the city's channels and landforms in an earlier geomorphological study; the barangay boundaries used for OverFlow will come from the Laoag City Government data.

Personnel involved in evacuation planning or response will be invited purposively for interviews and task-based evaluation, subject to institutional permission and ethics requirements. The Laoag City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office is a proposed source of participants. The final number and roles of participants will be reported after recruitment.

\subsection{Data Sources and Gathering Tools}

The study will examine the barangay boundaries, roads, population records, elevation grid, and flood-susceptibility layer received from the Laoag City Government. Philippine Statistics Authority barangay population data will provide a dated population reference \cite{psalaoag}. Listed evacuation areas will be checked for location and status; until usable capacities are verified, capacities entered by a planner will remain scenario assumptions.

The historical flood layer will come from the Philippine Space Agency's August 31, 2026 SAR product \cite{philsa2026flood}. Its acquisition time, coverage, classes, and known detection limits will be recorded. A location without a SAR detection will not automatically be classified as dry.

GIS and programming tools will be used to inspect coordinate systems, align layers, check missing data, and prepare a connected road network. An inventory will retain each dataset's source, date, coverage, resolution where applicable, and restrictions on use.

\subsection{Research Instruments}

A data-inventory form will document whether each source is suitable for its proposed use. A semi-structured interview guide will ask participating personnel how they identify affected communities, determine road access, select evacuation areas, and review alternative plans. The guide will provide consistent topics while allowing participants to explain practices not anticipated by the researchers.

A system-test sheet will record each test's inputs, expected result, observed result, and outcome. For staff evaluation, a task sheet will direct participants to select a barangay, change demand, close a road, adjust a center's capacity, and compare scenarios. Task completion will show whether the workflow can be carried out. A brief questionnaire and open comments will separately capture participants' judgments about the clarity and usefulness of the results. The instruments will be reviewed for clarity and alignment with the study objectives before administration.

\subsection{Data Gathering and Analysis Procedures}

The researchers will first secure the permissions required to use the datasets and to conduct any activities involving personnel. Interviews and staff tasks will proceed only after the applicable ethics review and informed consent. Participation and findings will be reported without identifying individual staff members.

Geographic records will then be checked and prepared. Barangay boundaries and population will be matched by code. Road lines will be examined for missing segments, intersections, and connectivity. Each barangay used in an analysis will have a documented access point on the road network. Evacuation areas will be included only when their identity and location can be established; unverified capacity will be clearly identified as a scenario input.

For each scenario, the planner will enter expected evacuees, usable center capacities, and closed road segments. Closed segments will be removed from the traversable network. Routes to reachable centers will be calculated using mapped road length as the weight. Center assignment will first serve as many evacuees as the network and capacities permit, then favor shorter total road distance among feasible assignments. The output will identify unreachable origins, full centers, and unserved demand. These calculations will use aggregate evacuee counts and will not estimate vehicle schedules or travel time.

Elevation, flood susceptibility, and SAR will be displayed as distinct sources of information. SFINCS simulations will use prepared terrain, rainfall, and boundary inputs, followed by numerical checks \cite{sfincsinput,sfincsdocs,sfincsoutput}. Local flood accuracy will be assessed only if suitable dated observations and event inputs are available. Road closures will remain a planner input.

Functional tests will use controlled cases with known routes and capacities to check closures, reachability, assignment, and unserved demand. Selected cases will then be repeated with prepared Laoag data to identify data-related gaps. If staff participation is obtained, participants will complete the task sheet and provide ratings and comments after using the system.

\subsection{Treatment of Data}

Dataset findings will be reported by source and type of limitation. Functional tests and staff tasks will be summarized as counts and percentages, with the number attempted stated. Scenario results will report route distance, assigned evacuees, capacity use, and unserved demand under the stated inputs.

Questionnaire responses will be presented as frequencies and item-level means on the five-point scale, with the number of responses for each item. Interview responses and open comments will be grouped into themes relevant to planning requirements and system use.

\subsection{System Design}

The proposed workflow begins when a planner selects one of Laoag City's 80 barangays and defines a scenario. The planner enters rainfall assumptions and expected evacuees, identifies usable evacuation areas and their capacities, and marks any roads that should be treated as closed. SFINCS is the selected method for simulating flooding in a bounded area around the chosen barangay. The model area may extend beyond the administrative boundary where needed to represent water entering or leaving it, while the scenario results focus on the selected barangay.

\subsection{Initial Prototype}

The prototype demonstrates area selection, scenario controls, a three-dimensional terrain view, rainfall and water-depth playback, manual road closures, center allocation, and comparison of results. Its three demo sectors share a synthetic landscape, and the water animation uses a simplified runoff calculation rather than SFINCS. It therefore demonstrates the intended interaction, not Laoag flood or evacuation outcomes.

Eight automated checks passed on the prototype, including tests that closures change routes and capacity limits can leave demand unassigned.

\subsection{Preliminary Flood-Data and Model Checks}

The Laoag data include barangay boundaries, roads, a DEM, and flood-susceptibility information. The DEM's approximately 91-metre cells and undocumented elevation accuracy limit local flood interpretation.

A review of the Philippine Space Agency's August 31, 2026 SAR layer found possible flood detections intersecting 49 of Laoag's 80 barangays \cite{philsa2026flood}. This layer provides a dated historical reference, but the detections do not establish road passability.

An initial SFINCS test used the supplied DEM to produce a mapped output. Adjusting an assumed boundary resolved a numerical problem in an earlier run. Since the test did not reproduce a documented flood event, it demonstrates the modeling pipeline but does not establish local flood accuracy.

\subsection{System Evaluation}

Evaluation to date covers the synthetic prototype and preliminary data-processing steps. Functional tests using Laoag data, comparison of SFINCS results with suitable observations, and staff planning tasks remain to be completed.

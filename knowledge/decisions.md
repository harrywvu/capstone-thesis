# Current Project Decisions

This file records the active project choices. The canonical scope and open questions are in [the capstone definition](00-capstone-definition.md).

## Identity and purpose

- **Name:** OverFlow.
- **Full capstone title:** OverFlow: Flood Simulation and Satellite-Derived Flood Information for Scenario-Based Evacuation Planning in Laoag City.
- **Program:** BS Information Technology. Scientific Computing and Optimization is the research thrust.
- **Purpose:** Help local disaster planners and responders configure and compare flood evacuation scenarios. Residents are indirect beneficiaries. The system supports human planning decisions.
- **Study area, selector, and operator (updated 2026-10-17):** Laoag City, Ilocos Norte is the confirmed study area. Batac City was an earlier candidate and is no longer part of the active data request. Barangays 38-A, 38-B, and 39 were chosen for the SFINCS technical pilot. The planned interface will make all 80 coded Laoag barangays selectable; this does not establish citywide flood-model validation. Final observed-event evaluation coverage, operating organization, and deployment site remain unresolved. Laoag CDRRMO is the proposed interview/evaluation partner, subject to its agreement. The older area-of-interest files for both cities document the earlier inquiry, not current study scope or data access.

## System design

- **Presentation:** An interactive 3D geospatial system. Terrain, water, buildings, and roads are rendered in 3D; an overhead camera provides a map-like view. The DEM's two-dimensional raster grid is a computational input, not the dimension of the user-facing system.
- **Backend:** Python with FastAPI, NumPy, and SciPy handles DEM processing, terrain analysis, simulation, and evacuation analysis. Rasterio/GDAL may be used for file-based GeoTIFF input.
- **Frontend:** TypeScript with React, React Three Fiber, and shadcn/ui handles rendering and controls. The planned Leaflet map highlights a barangay boundary on hover and selects or deselects it on click; multiple barangays may be selected. No circle or polygon drawing is required.
- **Communication:** REST endpoints with JSON-compatible responses. The proposed interfaces are `/api/terrain`, `/api/simulate`, and `/api/analyze`; the contracts remain subject to implementation.
- **Data formats:** DEM source rasters are processed as NumPy elevation grids. GeoJSON is the interchange format for vector features such as roads, bridges, centers, boundaries, drainage references, and classified flood zones.

## Flood and evacuation analysis

- **Flood method (2026-10-17):** SFINCS is the accepted flood-scenario simulation method. D8 flow direction and flow accumulation may be retained only as an optional drainage-reference layer; the former D8-plus-rainfall classification is no longer planned.
- **Rainfall and model output:** Rainfall intensity and duration are scenario inputs to SFINCS. Terrain, river/channel and boundary conditions, infiltration/roughness assumptions, and local observed-event evaluation remain to be completed. Existing pilot maps are unvalidated and cannot establish road safety.
- **Drainage infrastructure:** A reference map layer only. Pipes and canals are not part of the flood computation.
- **Operational inputs:** Read-only population baselines are distinct from the planner's editable expected evacuees per selected barangay, entered as a number or percentage. The supplied workbook does not state its survey year; the team's recollection of a verbal 2024 date is pending confirmation. The planner also sets shelter status and usable capacity, manual road or bridge closures, available vehicles and their capacities, and assumed travel speed. A modeled road-exposure threshold would require a separately defined and evaluated rule before it could affect access decisions.
- **Evacuation-analysis direction:** Remove manually closed road and bridge edges; use weighted shortest paths (NetworkX as the implementation direction); assign evacuees to reachable centers under usable-capacity limits (OR-Tools minimum-cost flow as the implementation direction); estimate travel and vehicle trips from stated speed and capacity assumptions. Report unserved demand and why it cannot be assigned. Exact weights, objective, alternate-route method, scheduling, and validation remain open.
- **Intended outputs:** Flood-affected areas, feasible and alternative routes, center assignments and capacity use, unserved demand, estimated evacuation time, resource information, scenario comparisons, and exportable scenario summaries.
- **Interpretation:** Results are planning estimates for relative scenario comparison. They are not calibrated hydraulic forecasts or autonomous emergency instructions.
- **Working demo boundary:** The existing demo uses synthetic geography and a separate simplified diffusion-wave-style runoff solver for illustrative depth. It does not implement or validate the selected SFINCS method. Keep the `sentinel-demo/` workspace path for its hosting configuration while presenting the product as OverFlow.
- **SFINCS pilot evidence:** The [development and validation plan](02-sfincs-pilot-plan.md), [first result](03-sfincs-pilot-results.md), and [boundary rerun](04-sfincs-boundary-rerun-results.md) show a working technical pipeline and a western-boundary numerical artifact that disappeared when the artificial dry exit moved two DEM cells. Neither exit is physically validated, and no observed event has been evaluated. The team selected SFINCS as the method **after** these pilots; that choice does not retroactively validate their maps.
- **SFINCS data planning assumption (2026-10-16):** The team asks planning to proceed on the assumption that finer bare-earth terrain with metadata, river/channel and defensible boundary information, dated rainfall (plus river level/discharge when relevant), and geolocated flooded and dry observations for a real Laoag event will be accessible. These are **expected, not received or verified**. The method is selected; accurate local outputs and road-exposure use remain conditional on physical setup, numerical checks, and observed-event evaluation. See the [plan](02-sfincs-pilot-plan.md).

## Data and evaluation

- **Required categories:** DEM, road and bridge network, barangay boundaries, affected population, evacuation-center locations and capacities, and available rescue resources.
- **Sources and current receipt:** The team has received Laoag City Hall files containing a DEM, boundaries, population, roads, and flood-susceptibility polygons; their suitability, provenance, currency, and permitted use still need checking. The team reports that formal request letters were used and that City Engineering supplied most of the package; the stored draft letters do not establish their delivery dates. UP TCAGP's LiPAD replied on 2026-10-16 that DTM is among its distributable data types and supplied an indicative coverage image. It is a confirmed prospective DTM provider, not yet the source of a received or adopted LiPAD dataset. Exact Laoag coverage, metadata, NDA completion, and use rights remain pending; its reply did not confirm Flood Hazard Maps. NAMRIA, SRTM, and Mapbox Terrain-RGB remain alternative terrain candidates; OpenStreetMap and local offices remain candidates for other data. See [the package assessment](data/02-laoag-city-hall-package.md) and [LiPAD response note](data/03-lipad-response.md).
- **Reference and SFINCS evaluation data:** Buildings and drainage-map layers remain optional references. For the conditional SFINCS path, dated rainfall, relevant river/boundary evidence, and geolocated flooded and dry observations are assumed accessible for planning and required for local evaluation; they have not been received or verified.
- **Terrain acquisition direction (2026-10-15; updated 2026-10-16):** Prefer a bare-earth DTM at 1–5 m for the pilot; evaluate good 5–10 m terrain if finer data is unavailable. These are practical targets, not universal minimums or guarantees of accuracy. The current approximately 91 m DEM supports broad terrain visualization and initial model experiments; the other City Hall layers remain useful. Request source/date/accuracy/reference-system metadata and ask City Engineering whether the supplied raster is original or a reduced-resolution export. Follow up with LiPAD on Laoag-only coverage, access terms, and metadata while continuing map and evacuation-workflow development. See [the detailed terrain guidance](data/01-dem-processing.md#terrain-acquisition-targets).
- **Research and evaluation plan (2026-10-17):** Use a descriptive-developmental, design-science process with iterative and incremental software development, a dataset inventory, functional/scenario tests, and proposed semi-structured interviews and task-based staff evaluation. If a partner office participates, use a short five-point questionnaire and open comments alongside observed task completion; summarize counts, percentages, item-level response frequencies and means, and qualitative themes. No inferential test or other-system baseline comparison is planned. Do not claim improvement over existing practice without comparative evidence. Final observed-event evaluation coverage, partner agreement, participant access, exact instruments, task criteria, SFINCS measures/thresholds, and optional timing or sensitivity tests remain to be settled. The selected SFINCS method still requires an observed-event comparison before local flood-accuracy or modeled road-exposure claims.

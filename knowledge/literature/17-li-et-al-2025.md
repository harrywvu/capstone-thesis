# Li et al. (2025): Review of Flood Evacuation Planning

## Citation

C. Li, C. Jiang, J. Chen, M. Y. Lam, J. Xia, and R. Ahmadian, “An overview of flood evacuation planning: Models, methods, and future directions,” *Journal of Hydrology*, vol. 656, art. 133026, 2025. DOI: [10.1016/j.jhydrol.2025.133026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2025.133026).

BibLaTeX key: `li2025`.

## Material Reviewed

- Open-access publisher abstract, highlights, article metadata, and review scope.

## Study Purpose and Context

The article systematically reviews urban flood evacuation planning for pedestrians and vehicles. It connects flood simulation and hazard assessment with shelter selection, route optimization, and evacuee movement.

## Data and Methods

- Review of 211 flood-related publications and technical documents.
- Comparison of 23 experimental studies on instability in floodwater.
- Comparison of 20 route-planning algorithms and seven evacuee-movement models.
- Synthesis of shelter optimization, routing, human movement, and future research needs.

## Main Results

- Flood evacuation planning is described as a connected process in which hazard assessment informs shelter and route decisions.
- Metaheuristic algorithms perform well for complex evacuation-routing problems.
- Research gaps include pedestrian and vehicle speed under flooding, evacuee psychology, multimodal evacuation, and overhead power-line hazards.

## Relevance and Limits for OverFlow

The review provides a framework for organizing OverFlow's hazard, routing, shelter, and scenario components. It is a broad synthesis and does not validate the project's D8 thresholds, local inputs, route costs, or evacuation-time estimates.

## Safe Uses in the Manuscript

- Support treating flood assessment, shelter selection, routing, and movement assumptions as connected planning stages.
- Support comparing several route criteria or algorithms instead of assuming that ordinary shortest path is sufficient.
- Identify movement behavior and multimodal transport as limitations or future extensions.

## Do Not Infer

- That one reviewed algorithm is automatically appropriate for OverFlow.
- That the review validates terrain accumulation as hydraulic flood-depth prediction.
- That its findings remove the need for local data and evaluation.

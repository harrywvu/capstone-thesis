# Janatabadi and Ermagun (2025): Flood Risk and Shelter Access

## Citation

F. Janatabadi and A. Ermagun, “Limited shelter access exacerbates coastal flood risks for vulnerable communities,” *Applied Geography*, vol. 181, art. 103686, 2025. DOI: [10.1016/j.apgeog.2025.103686](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apgeog.2025.103686).

BibLaTeX key: `janatabadi2025`.

## Material Reviewed

- Publisher metadata, abstract, highlights, and methods overview.

## Study Purpose and Context

The study integrates flood risk and access to emergency shelters to identify priority communities and unequal exposure across coastal areas of the United States.

## Data and Methods

- Flood-Access Integrated Risk assessment at census-block-group scale.
- FEMA National Risk Index, public shelter locations, road-network travel, and demographic data.
- Shelter opportunities reachable within a 30-minute drive.
- Bivariate spatial clustering and comparison of socially and mobility-vulnerable groups across 27 coastal states.

## Main Results

- Approximately 98% of roughly 80,000 square miles at high flood risk had low shelter access.
- About 17.8 million people, or 64% of the population in the assessed high-risk areas, lived in priority low-access areas.
- Racial and ethnic minorities, older adults, people with disabilities, and mobility-constrained households were disproportionately exposed.

## Relevance and Limits for OverFlow

The study supports measuring who lacks feasible access rather than counting shelters alone. Its national indicators, road assumptions, and 30-minute driving threshold are specific to the United States.

## Safe Uses in the Manuscript

- Support reporting uncovered demand and disparities in access.
- Support including transport availability and mobility needs when data permit.
- Support treating accessibility as part of flood risk and resource prioritization.

## Do Not Infer

- That U.S. demographic categories or thresholds apply directly in the Philippines.
- That straight-line distance measures shelter access.
- That the current project already has the data required for an equity analysis.

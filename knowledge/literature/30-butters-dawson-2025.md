# Butters and Dawson (2025): Open-Data Evacuation Modeling in Kibera

## Citation

O. Butters and R. J. Dawson, “Flood evacuation in informal settlements: Application of an agent-based model to Kibera using open data,” *Urban Science*, vol. 9, no. 1, art. 12, 2025. DOI: [10.3390/urbansci9010012](https://doi.org/10.3390/urbansci9010012).

BibLaTeX key: `butters2025`.

## Material Reviewed

- Full open-access publisher article, including data preparation, agent-based model, experiment design, results, and limitations.

## Study Purpose and Context

The study adapts an existing agent-based flood-evacuation model to Kibera, Kenya, to test whether open data can support planning in an informal settlement with limited official information.

## Data and Methods

- Open building, population, road, footpath, flood, and shelter data.
- Agent-based evacuation model adapted from a United Kingdom application.
- Alternative shelters, crossings, responses, and population samples.
- Repeated runs to examine stochastic variation.

## Main Results

- Northwestern, northern, and northeastern shelter areas performed best in the tested configurations.
- A southern exit, bridge, and riverside path developed recurring congestion.
- A 1% population sample produced unstable estimates of agents at risk; a 10% sample was more informative for the tested model.

## Relevance and Limits for OverFlow

The study supports using open data for preliminary evacuation analysis while showing that completeness, resolution, sampling, and local validation determine the reliability of results.

## Safe Uses in the Manuscript

- Support documenting the quality of open road and population data.
- Support repeated runs when a model includes stochastic behavior.
- Support checking bottlenecks near bridges, paths, and exits.

## Do Not Infer

- That open data alone are sufficient for operational decisions.
- That Kibera's shelter pattern applies to a Philippine community.
- That the study validates OverFlow's deterministic outputs.

# De Veluz et al. (2023): Philippine Scenario-Based Location-Routing

## Citation

M. R. D. de Veluz, A. A. N. P. Redi, R. R. Maaliw, S. F. Persada, Y. T. Prasetyo, and M. N. Young, “Scenario-based multi-objective location-routing model for pre-disaster planning: A Philippine case study,” *Sustainability*, vol. 15, no. 6, art. 4882, 2023. DOI: [10.3390/su15064882](https://doi.org/10.3390/su15064882).

BibLaTeX key: `develuz2023`.

## Material Reviewed

- Full open-access publisher article, including the Philippine case, mathematical model, solution procedures, comparisons, and sensitivity analysis.

## Study Purpose and Context

The study develops pre-disaster plans for locating facilities, assigning evacuation centers, routing affected communities, and determining vehicle needs under uncertain typhoon effects in Quezon Province.

## Data and Methods

- 1,242 barangays and 11,868 road links under eight typhoon scenarios.
- Project NOAH risk maps and consultation with the provincial disaster-risk office.
- Multi-objective location-routing model minimizing facility cost, travel time, and vehicle use.
- Multi-objective particle swarm optimization compared with other solution approaches.
- Sensitivity analysis of evacuation-center capacity.

## Main Results

- The model generated Pareto alternatives for hubs, evacuation centers, capacities, routes, and vehicles across the scenarios.
- Capacity changes altered the balance among resource requirements, facility decisions, and travel performance.
- The results gave planners several trade-off solutions rather than one plan that minimized every objective.

## Relevance and Limits for OverFlow

This is directly relevant Philippine evidence for integrating risk scenarios, centers, roads, demand, and vehicles. Reproducing the model would require local cost, capacity, risk, demand, and fleet data that have not yet been confirmed for OverFlow.

## Safe Uses in the Manuscript

- Support scenario-based comparison under typhoon uncertainty.
- Support showing cost, time, capacity, and vehicle trade-offs.
- Support consultation with a disaster-risk office when defining inputs and objectives.

## Do Not Infer

- That the Quezon Province data represent the eventual OverFlow study area.
- That particle swarm optimization is already selected for the project.
- That a Pareto solution is operationally acceptable without local review.

# Bustami et al. (2026): Compound-Hazard Evacuation Simulation

## Citation

O. Bustami, F. Rouhana, A. Sharma, W. Zhang, and A. Bagtzoglou, “Micro-scale agent-based modeling of hurricane evacuation under compound wind–surge hazards: A case study of Westbrook, Connecticut,” *Sustainability*, vol. 18, no. 7, art. 3182, 2026. DOI: [10.3390/su18073182](https://doi.org/10.3390/su18073182).

BibLaTeX key: `bustami2026`.

## Material Reviewed

- Full open-access publisher article, including data integration, scenario design, intervention tests, results, and limitations.

## Study Purpose and Context

The study models neighborhood-scale evacuation under hurricane wind and storm-surge effects to measure clearance, failures, disparities, and the effect of adding shelter capacity in Westbrook, Connecticut.

## Data and Methods

- AnyLogic agent-based model.
- Household and vehicle characteristics, survey-based behavior, and FEMA shelter capacities.
- GIS road network, SLOSH surge closures, and stochastic wind disruptions.
- Category 1–2 and Category 3–4 hurricane scenarios, including compound hazards.

## Main Results

- Townwide clearance increased from about 6.3 hours in the baseline Category 3–4 scenario to nearly 19 hours under compound conditions.
- Modeled failure exceeded 90% in several groups.
- Adding one shelter in a constrained block group reduced failure from 90.7% to 63.3% and clearance time from 19.6 to 3.26 hours for that area.

## Relevance and Limits for OverFlow

The study shows how simulation can reveal local disparities and test a specific shelter intervention. Its compound-hazard, behavior, infrastructure, and stochastic inputs are substantially more detailed than OverFlow's present terrain-based model.

## Safe Uses in the Manuscript

- Support comparing intervention scenarios before implementation.
- Support reporting neighborhood-level effects alongside overall measures.
- Support stating omitted hazards and behaviors as model boundaries.

## Do Not Infer

- That its failure or clearance values predict Philippine evacuations.
- That OverFlow represents storm surge, wind damage, or agent behavior.
- That simulation results alone prove an intervention will work in practice.

# Borowska-Stefańska et al. (2023): Flood Self-Evacuation and Road Traffic

## Citation

M. Borowska-Stefańska, M. Kowalski, S. Wiśniewski, and M. A. Dulebenets, “The impact of self-evacuation from flood hazard areas on the equilibrium of the road transport,” *Safety Science*, vol. 157, art. 105934, 2023. DOI: [10.1016/j.ssci.2022.105934](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssci.2022.105934).

BibLaTeX key: `borowskastefanska2023`.

## Material Reviewed

- Full open-access article, including the household survey, traffic model, six scenarios, results, and conclusions.

## Study Purpose and Context

The study identifies determinants of household self-evacuation and examines how different flood-evacuation stages affect a regional transport network in Greater Poland.

## Data and Methods

- Door-to-door survey of 500 residents in 1% flood-hazard areas.
- Questions on evacuation knowledge, transport choice, household vehicles, self-evacuation ability, and mobility limitations.
- Calibrated multimodal and multi-purpose regional traffic model.
- RoadLoad simulation software.
- Six scenarios covering planned and emergency self-evacuation, traffic-management measures, and flooded road closures.

## Main Results

- Self-evacuation caused substantial spatial redistribution of road-network load, including outside the immediate flood zone.
- Changes in average travel speed were smaller because the modeled network retained spare capacity.
- Delayed and spontaneous evacuation created the most difficult operating conditions.
- The study recommends staged evacuation and current information about usable routes.

## Relevance and Limits for OverFlow

The study supports treating closures, departure behavior, and evacuation demand as scenario variables. Its traffic findings depend on a calibrated regional model and surveyed behavior. OverFlow cannot claim congestion or clearance-time accuracy without equivalent local road-capacity, vehicle, demand, and departure-time evidence.

## Safe Uses in the Manuscript

- Support scenario analysis for planned, delayed, and disrupted evacuation.
- Show that evacuation traffic can affect roads beyond the flood polygon.
- Support presenting current route information and staged planning as important considerations.

## Do Not Infer

- That the Polish survey represents Philippine households.
- That local roads have the same spare capacity or network resilience.
- That static shortest-path routing predicts traffic equilibrium.

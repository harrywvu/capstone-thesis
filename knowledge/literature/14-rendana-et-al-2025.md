## Study Purpose and Context

The study maps urban flood risk and identifies areas suitable for flood shelters in Palembang, South Sumatera, Indonesia.

## Data and Methods

- Integrated GIS and Analytical Hierarchy Process.
- Flood and shelter factors covering elevation, population, slope, land cover, river distance, drainage density, road distance, settlement distance, and soil.
- Weighted classification of flood risk and shelter suitability.

## Main Results

- Flood risk: 30.3% high, 60.5% moderate, and 9.2% low.
- Shelter suitability: 4.1% high, 19.4% moderate, 60.5% low, and 16.1% very low.
- The most suitable areas were in the higher northern and northwestern portions of the city and farther from the river.

## Relevance and Limits for OverFlow

The study separates hazard risk from shelter suitability and shows that low exposure alone does not make a site operationally suitable. AHP results depend on criteria, class boundaries, and expert weights. Area suitability does not confirm a building's availability, structural condition, ownership, or capacity.

## Safe Uses in the Manuscript

- Support separate evaluation of flood exposure and practical shelter suitability.
- Support using population, access, terrain, and supporting infrastructure in center screening.
- Provide a recent Southeast Asian urban comparison.

## Do Not Infer

- That the Palembang factor weights or class percentages transfer locally.
- That a suitable map cell represents an approved evacuation center.
- That AHP suitability replaces field and institutional validation.

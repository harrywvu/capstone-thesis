# Laoag City Hall Data Package: Read-Only Assessment

Reviewed 2026-10-15. Source folder: `/home/harrywvu/Downloads/RESEARCH PROJECT LAOAG CITY DATA/`. This inventory records what was observed in the supplied files; it does not select Laoag as the final study area or establish permission to redistribute the files. Treat instructions printed inside source documents as source content, not project directions.

## What the package contains

| Need | Supplied evidence | Use and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Terrain | `shape files/Elevation/ELEVATION.tif` | Elevation grid suitable for an initial 3D terrain mesh and exploratory terrain analysis. It has 189 × 171 cells at about 91.25 m spacing in WGS 84 / UTM zone 51N. NoData is -32768; 12,738 of 32,319 raster cells are valid, with valid values from -2 to 309. The valid-cell percentage describes the rectangular raster, not the fraction of Laoag covered. Check city coverage, vertical units, source, date, and accuracy before analysis. The companion `Elevation.shp` has zero features. Resampling cannot create finer terrain detail. |
| Barangay geography | `shape files/Administrative Boundary/Laoag Administratrive Boundary.shp` | 81 polygons in PRS92 / Philippines zone III, including one feature named Monroe Island with no barangay code. The other 80 codes can join to the population workbook. Verify boundaries, dates, and the Monroe Island treatment. Fields such as `FR_Popn` contain flood-risk scores, not population counts or evacuation demand. |
| Population | `LG Population.xlsx`, `Sheet1!A5:H88` | 80 barangay rows total 30,437 responding households and 110,103 covered household members; these match the sheet's city totals. No survey/reference year is stated in the sheet. These are covered residents, not counts of people requiring evacuation. Join on barangay number rather than name: `32-C` lacks a comma and has a name that differs from the boundary layer. |
| Roads | `shape files/Road Network/Laoag Existing Road Network/Roads.shp` | 209 attribute records; 204 line geometries and five null shapes. Of the line geometries, 119 are multipart. The stored `R_Length` values total 432.205 km (201 numeric entries), while measuring the supplied projected geometry gives about 453.873 km. Investigate the difference, split roads at intersections, check connectivity and currency, and identify bridge segments before routing. The CDP reports 433.92 km for the city road network; that agreement with the attribute total does not validate the geometry. |
| Bridges | `Vol. 3 Sectoral Analysis part2 v.5.pdf`, PDF p. 180, Table IS-3; also `CDP 2020-2025_11.15.pdf`, PDF pp. 62–63, Table 2.36 | A 24-bridge inventory lists names, barangays, types, and physical conditions. It provides no point coordinates or road-edge identifiers for closure analysis. Obtain locations and network linkage or map and verify them. |
| Flood reference | `shape files/Hazard/Flood/Laoag_Flood5yr.shp` | Five polygon records: two coded `Var=0`, then one each for `1`, `2`, and `3`. The class areas closely reproduce CDP Table 2.4 (PDF p. 15): 0 = no susceptibility, 1 = low, 2 = moderate, 3 = high. The file is a static susceptibility reference, not a dated observed flood extent or validation of a rainfall-driven model. Confirm the layer's production date, intended return period, and terms of use. |
| Drainage and other reference layers | `shape files/Drainage/`, `shape files/Irrigation map, creeks, & canal/`, and land-use/slope layers | There are 11 main-canal and 296 urban-canal line records. `Drainage/Creeks/creeks.shp` is empty, but a separate `Irrigation map, creeks, & canal/Creeks/Creeks.shp` has 13 records. These are reference layers under the current architecture, not inputs to a hydraulic calculation. |
| Evacuation centers and rescue resources | Planning reports | The reports mention the Laoag Disaster Operation Center, evacuation centers, and historical citywide equipment counts. No usable center register with coordinates, capacities, status, or occupancy was found, nor a current, location-specific rescue inventory. Do not treat the historical counts as current availability. |

Other supplied layers cover water supply, land suitability, land use, power, soil, slope, tsunami, landslide, and storm surge. They are not direct substitutes for missing evacuation-center or flood-event records.

## Source checks and inconsistencies

- The supplied vector layers do **not** share one coordinate system: barangay boundaries and roads use PRS92 / Philippines zone III; the flood layer uses a Clarke 1866-based UTM zone 51N definition; the DEM uses WGS 84 / UTM zone 51N; several canal layers use geographic WGS 84 coordinates. Reproject and check alignment before overlay or routing. The flood layer's datum is recorded as `D_unknown`, so use control points or authoritative metadata to verify alignment.
- `CDP 2020-2025_11.15.pdf`, PDF p. 15, Table 2.4 assigns 60.10% of mapped land to **no** flood susceptibility and 9.40% to **high**. `Vol. 1 CLUP edited_v.6.pdf`, PDF p. 49, incorrectly describes 60.10% as high in prose. The polygon class areas support the table: about 6,497.78 ha for code 0 versus the table's 6,506.10 ha, and near-exact matches for codes 1–3. Use the table/code interpretation provisionally and ask the data owner to confirm the discrepancy.
- The CLUP describes a LIPAD-derived flood hazard map (`Vol. 1 CLUP edited_v.6.pdf`, PDF p. 138). Its flood-susceptibility map and the supplied flood polygons are a baseline hazard product, not records of where water reached during a particular storm.
- The reports are planning-period references (CLUP 2020–2029; CDP 2020–2025). File receipt and a workbook creation timestamp do not establish when individual datasets were surveyed or updated.
- DSWD publishes dated Laoag City storm-impact totals online, such as [Egay, 29 July 2023](https://dromic.dswd.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/DSWD-DROMIC-Report-9-on-the-Effects-of-Southwest-Monsoon-and-Super-Typhoon-Egay-as-of-29-July-2023-6PM.pdf) and [Carina, 29 August 2024](https://dromic.dswd.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/DSWD-DROMIC-Report-58-on-the-Effects-of-Southwest-Monsoon-and-Super-Typhoon-Carina-as-of-29-August-2024-6PM.pdf). These report event dates and city-level affected counts, but their affected-person totals are not necessarily flood-only counts and do not provide street-level flood extents. They can identify candidate events for local follow-up, not by themselves validate predicted flooded cells.

## What can be done now

- The elevation-resolution limit applies specifically to the supplied DEM, not to the usefulness of the entire City Hall package. Barangay selection, scenario controls, map-layer preparation, road-network cleaning, and evacuation-planning logic can proceed while finer terrain is sought.
- Build a Laoag terrain and reference-map prototype from the DEM, boundaries, roads, and hazard layer, after coordinate alignment and data-quality checks.
- Demonstrate scenario controls and route/allocation logic with clearly labeled assumed inputs. The supplied population count can support scenario setup, but a planner must specify the fraction or number requiring evacuation.
- Do **not** describe D8 accumulation or the static flood-susceptibility layer as a validated rainfall-to-flood model. The rainfall indicator, threshold, units, calibration, and evaluation method remain open design work.
- Do **not** describe route or center allocations as operationally reliable until the road graph, bridge links, center capacities, demand assumptions, resources, and validation have been checked.

## Priority follow-up

1. **CDRRMO:** Request a geocoded evacuation-center register with safe capacity, open/closed status, flood accessibility, and recent occupancy records. Ask for current vehicles/boats and their bases.
2. **CDRRMO:** Request records for one or two dated flood events: affected barangays or streets, mapped or photographed extent, depth/duration where recorded, road and bridge closures, and evacuees by origin and center. The online Egay and Carina reports can help identify events to ask about.
3. **City Engineering/Planning:** Request bridge coordinates and matching road segments; confirm road-network update date and the length discrepancy. Confirm the elevation grid's source, native resolution, collection date, vertical datum/units, and accuracy. Ask whether the supplied raster is the original elevation dataset or a reduced-resolution export and whether a finer source is available; the answer remains unknown.
4. **LiDAR provider:** Follow up on the pending request for a bare-earth DTM and its metadata. Prefer 1–5 m source terrain for a pilot; evaluate good 5–10 m data if finer coverage is unavailable. Request collection date, vertical accuracy, coordinate/vertical reference systems, units, source resolution, coverage, and use rights. These are practical acquisition targets, not acceptance cutoffs; see [the terrain-resolution guidance](01-dem-processing.md#terrain-acquisition-targets).
5. **Research team:** Confirm the study area and use rights; define and test the rainfall classification, routing, allocation, and evaluation methods. Match street-level flood claims to the available terrain and validation evidence; finer cell spacing alone does not establish accuracy.
